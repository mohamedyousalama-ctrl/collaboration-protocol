# **1.  Motivation: When Intent Fidelity and Clinical Safety Diverge**

The Collaboration Protocol (CP) is designed to maximize Intent Fidelity — ensuring AI outputs precisely match human-specified intent. In most domains, higher IFI unambiguously represents better outcomes. However, empirical pilot data from the IntentHealth Comparator instrument (Sessions 1–2, March 2026) revealed a structurally important edge case unique to health contexts:

| **Observed Anomaly — Session 1 (Chest Tightness):** CP-Compliant Claude (IFI = 0.81) produced a pulmonary-focused response after the user resolved a Scope IPP toward "tightness when breathing deeply." Raw Claude (IFI = 0.51) produced a cardiac-first response without disambiguation. External clinical evaluation (GPT-4o) rated the lower-IFI response as more medically appropriate for a 38-year-old smoker with exertional chest symptoms. |
| --- |

This anomaly does not invalidate CP — it reveals that in health contexts, two distinct optimization objectives can diverge:

| **Objective** | **Optimized By** | **Metric** | **Can Conflict?** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Intent Fidelity | CP Protocol (ICD + IPP + IFI) | IFI score | Yes |
| Clinical Safety | Medical best practice | Adverse outcome avoidance | Yes |
| User Satisfaction | Perceived response quality | SS rating 1–7 | Partial |

*Table 1. Three optimization objectives in health AI — intent fidelity and clinical safety may diverge when user-resolved IPPs lead away from clinically indicated caution.*

The Safety Floor construct resolves this tension by establishing a **non-negotiable minimum safety layer** that is appended to all health responses, independent of IPP resolution pathway. It preserves CP's core principle (human intent authority) while ensuring clinical safety is never a casualty of personalization.

# **2.  Formal Definition**

## **2.1  Safety Floor (SF)**

The Safety Floor is defined as:

| **Definition 1 — Safety Floor (SF):** A domain-specific, clinically validated set of safety assertions SF = {s₁, s₂, ..., sₙ} that are appended to every health AI response regardless of ICD setting, IPP resolution pathway, or user-specified intent constraints. Safety Floor assertions are not generated by the AI — they are pre-validated, version-controlled clinical content approved by qualified medical reviewers. |
| --- |

The Safety Floor operates orthogonally to the CP intent pipeline. It is not an IPP. It is not subject to user disambiguation. It cannot be suppressed by low ICD settings. It is the clinical equivalent of a non-negotiable disclosure requirement.

## **2.2  Relationship to Existing CP Constructs**

| **CP Construct** | **Governs** | **User Controllable?** | **SF Relationship** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ICD (0.0–1.0) | How many IPPs are surfaced | Yes — user chooses | SF bypasses ICD entirely |
| IPP | Semantic ambiguity points | Yes — user resolves | SF is independent of IPP choices |
| IFI | Intent fidelity score | Indirectly (via SS) | SF does not affect IFI calculation |
| Safety Floor (SF) | Minimum safety content | No — always present | New construct, this document |

*Table 2. Safety Floor as a fifth CP construct, operating independently of the existing ICD/IPP/IFI pipeline.*

# **3.  Architecture: The Modified CP Interaction Flow**

## **3.1  Standard CP Flow (Existing)**

- Intent Expression: User articulates query

- Pivot Detection: System identifies IPPs

- Pivot Surfacing: IPPs presented per ICD setting

- Pivot Resolution: User resolves ambiguity

- Constrained Generation: AI produces output respecting resolved intent

- Output Delivery

## **3.2  CP-Health Flow (Safety Floor Extension)**

- Intent Expression: User articulates query

- Domain Classification: System detects health domain → SF profile selected

- Pivot Detection: System identifies IPPs

- Pivot Surfacing: IPPs presented per ICD setting

- Pivot Resolution: User resolves ambiguity

- Constrained Generation: AI produces output respecting resolved intent

- Safety Floor Injection: SF assertions appended — non-negotiable, version-stamped

- Output Delivery: [personalized CP response] + [SF block]

| **Key Architectural Principle:** Steps 1–6 are unchanged — CP's intent fidelity pipeline operates exactly as specified. Step 7 is additive, never substitutive. The Safety Floor does not replace or override the CP response; it augments it with a clinically non-negotiable layer. |

# **4.  Safety Floor Content Specification**

## **4.1  SF Profile Types**

Safety Floor content is not generic — it is organized into domain-specific profiles. Each profile is activated by domain classification at Step 2 of the CP-Health flow.

| **SF Profile** | **Activation Trigger** | **Core SF Assertions** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SF-CARDIAC | Chest symptoms + exertion + age 35+ or smoker | Cardiac red flags; ECG/stress test recommendation; emergency criteria |
| SF-RESPIRATORY | Breathing symptoms + smoker or occupational exposure | COPD/asthma red flags; spirometry recommendation; emergency criteria |
| SF-GENERAL-QUIT | Smoking cessation queries | NRT options; physician consultation; quit line resources |
| SF-EMERGENCY | Any query matching acute symptom pattern | Emergency service numbers; do-not-delay message; symptom escalation criteria |
| SF-BASELINE | All health queries (fallback) | "This is educational only"; clinician consultation recommendation; disclaimer |

*Table 3. Safety Floor profiles and their activation conditions. Multiple profiles may activate simultaneously; assertions are deduplicated.*

## **4.2  SF-CARDIAC Content Example**

The following represents the approved content for the SF-CARDIAC profile, as would be established through clinical review:

| **⚠  SF-CARDIAC Safety Floor — Required Content** Regardless of the response above, the following applies to your situation: Seek emergency care immediately if you experience: chest pain spreading to arm, jaw, or back; shortness of breath at rest; dizziness or sweating with chest symptoms; symptoms lasting more than a few minutes. With your profile (age 38, smoking, exercise-triggered symptoms), cardiac causes must be ruled out by a clinician — this is standard medical triage protocol. Recommended: ECG and clinical assessment within days, not weeks. *SF-CARDIAC v1.0 │ Clinically reviewed │ Educational information only — not a substitute for professional medical evaluation.* |

# **5.  IFI Extension: The SF-Augmented Formula**

## **5.1  The Problem with Standard IFI in Health Contexts**

The standard IFI formula measures how well output matches user-specified intent:

**IFI = 0.4(SA) + 0.3(CP) + 0.3(SS)**   (Eq. 1 — Standard IFI)

In health contexts, a response can achieve high IFI while omitting clinically critical safety content — because the user never specified that content as a constraint. The SF extension addresses this by introducing a Safety Compliance Score.

## **5.2  Safety Compliance Score (SCS)**

The Safety Compliance Score measures whether the response satisfies the applicable Safety Floor profile:

**SCS = (SF assertions present) / (SF assertions required)   ∈ [0, 1]**   (Eq. 2)

SCS = 1.0 if all required SF assertions are present in the response. SCS = 0.0 if no SF assertions are present. SCS is binary per assertion — each required assertion is either present or absent.

## **5.3  IFI-Health Formula**

For health domain interactions, IFI is computed using the SF-augmented formula:

**IFI-H = 0.35(SA) + 0.25(CP) + 0.25(SS) + 0.15(SCS)**   (Eq. 3 — IFI-Health)

| **Component** | **Weight** | **What It Measures** | **Range** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SA — Semantic Alignment | 0.35 | Output matches user-specified intent meaning | 0–1 (normalized from 1–7) |
| CP — Constraint Preservation | 0.25 | Proportion of stated constraints satisfied | 0–1 |
| SS — Subjective Satisfaction | 0.25 | User-reported satisfaction with response | 0–1 (normalized from 1–7) |
| SCS — Safety Compliance | 0.15 | SF assertions present / SF assertions required | 0–1 |

*Table 4. IFI-Health component weights. SA weight is slightly reduced from 0.40 to accommodate SCS. CP and SS weights each reduced by 0.05 to maintain sum = 1.0.*

| **Weight Justification:** SCS receives 0.15 — lower than any other component — deliberately. The Safety Floor is a minimum standard, not the primary measure of response quality. A response that achieves SCS = 1.0 but fails on SA, CP, and SS is still a poor response. Conversely, a response that achieves SA = 1.0, CP = 1.0, SS = 1.0, SCS = 0.0 fails the health standard entirely, yielding IFI-H = 0.60 — a structural penalty that cannot be compensated by high intent fidelity alone. |
| --- |

# **6.  Protocol Law Extension: Law 8**

The seven existing CP Protocol Laws govern intent authority, ambiguity handling, traceability, and human override. The Safety Floor construct requires one additional law:

| **Law 8: Domain Safety Non-Negotiability** In safety-critical domains (health, legal, financial), a domain-validated Safety Floor shall be appended to all responses, independent of ICD setting, IPP resolution pathway, or user-specified intent constraints. The Safety Floor is not subject to user suppression, ICD calibration, or semantic personalization. It is the minimum acceptable output for domain-critical interactions. **Rationale: **CP's core principle (human intent authority) is preserved for all semantic decisions. Law 8 carves out a narrow exception: clinical safety content is non-negotiable because the cost of omission is potentially irreversible patient harm. This is consistent with existing medical disclosure law and informed consent doctrine. |
| --- |

# **7.  Audit Log Extension for Safety Floor**

The existing CP Audit Log (Law 6 — Pivot Traceability) must be extended to record Safety Floor activation and compliance. Each SF event generates two log entries:

| **Log Entry Type** | **Recorded When** | **Fields Logged** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SF_ACTIVATED | Domain classification triggers an SF profile | Timestamp, session ID, SF profile name, SF version, activation trigger |
| SF_INJECTED | SF assertions are appended to the response | Timestamp, profile name, assertion count, SCS score, response length before/after injection |
| SF_VIOLATION | Response delivered without required SF content | Timestamp, missing assertions, severity classification (Critical/Significant) |

*Table 6. Safety Floor audit log entry types. SF_VIOLATION entries are automatically flagged for clinical governance review in enterprise deployments.*

# **8.  Research Implications and Future Work**

## **8.1  New Testable Hypothesis**

The Safety Floor construct generates one primary testable hypothesis:

| **H5 (Safety-Fidelity Trade-off):** In health domain interactions, optimizing for IFI (standard) and optimizing for clinical safety are not equivalent objectives. Specifically: H5a: CP-compliant responses will achieve higher IFI than raw responses but may achieve lower clinical safety ratings when user-resolved IPPs lead toward non-cardiac interpretations of exertional chest symptoms. H5b: IFI-Health (SF-augmented formula) will reduce but not eliminate the discrepancy between intent fidelity and clinical safety ratings. H5c: The Safety Floor construct, when implemented, will achieve SCS ≥ 0.90 without reducing user-rated SS (Subjective Satisfaction). |
| --- |

## **8.2  Validation Study Design**

H5 can be tested by extending Study 3 (ICD Effect on Collaboration Quality) with a health domain arm:

- Add SF-compliant condition (4th ICD condition: ICD + SF) to Study 3 design

- Measure IFI and IFI-Health separately for all conditions

- Add clinical expert rating panel: 3 physicians rate each response for clinical appropriateness

- Primary analysis: correlation between IFI, IFI-Health, and clinical expert rating

- Prediction: IFI-Health will correlate more strongly with clinical expert ratings than standard IFI

## **8.3  Limitations of This Specification**

- SF content requires ongoing clinical review — this document specifies the architecture, not the approved clinical content

- SF profile activation relies on domain classification accuracy — misclassification could suppress or incorrectly trigger profiles

- SCS scoring requires pre-defined assertion checklists per SF profile — these must be validated by clinical reviewers before deployment

- The 0.15 SCS weight in IFI-Health is a theoretical estimate; sensitivity analysis is required

- This specification applies to text-based health AI; multimodal systems require separate SF architecture
