dix A: Reviewer Objection Matrix

Anticipated objections and prepared responses for peer review:

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| **"This is just prompt engineering"** | Prompt engineering addresses input quality; CP governs interaction timing and ambiguity resolution authority. These are orthogonal interventions. |
| **"This limits AI autonomy"** | Autonomy is user-governed via ICD. Users can choose full autonomy (ICD=0) or full control (ICD=1). CP provides the mechanism, not the mandate. |
| **"Too theoretical without implementation"** | All constructs are operationalized with measurement protocols. Studies are specified for replication. This follows standard pre-implementation research practice. |
| **"Not scalable"** | Explicit intent scales better than implicit correction. Post-hoc revision cycles are costlier than upfront clarification. |
| **"Users don't want this much control"** | ICD is adjustable and user-chosen. The claim is not that all users want high control, but that the option should exist and its effects are measurable. |
| **"How is this different from HITL?"** | HITL addresses task oversight; CP addresses semantic authority. HITL asks "should the AI do this?"; CP asks "what does the user mean by this?" |
| **"IPPs are too subjective"** | Study 2 directly tests IPP reliability. If κ > 0.7, subjectivity is bounded within acceptable research standards. |
| **"No real-world validation"** | Acknowledged as a limitation. Phase 3 addresses this explicitly. The contribution is the framework and initial validation, not full deployment. |
| **"Safety framing is overblown"** | We cite specific harms: accountability gaps, untraceable decisions, user disempowerment. These are documented problems in AI deployment literature. |
| **"This is just speculation"** | It is falsifiable speculation with ready-to-run validation protocols. That distinguishes it from pure opinion. |

# Appendix B: Quick-Start Study Protocol

Complete protocol for conducting first empirical study in 30 days.

## B.1 Materials Checklist
- Prolific account with $1,500 balance
- Survey platform (Qualtrics, Google Forms, or Typeform)
- Screen recording capability (optional but recommended)
- Spreadsheet for transcript coding
- IPP Codebook (see Appendix C)

## B.2 Task Set (10 Tasks with Known Ambiguity)

1. **"Write a recommendation letter for my colleague"** — Ambiguous: tone, length, specific achievements
2. **"Summarize this article"** — Ambiguous: length, audience, key points selection
3. **"Help me plan a meeting"** — Ambiguous: format, attendees, duration, agenda depth
4. **"Analyze this data"** — Ambiguous: analysis type, visualization, depth
5. **"Draft an email to my team"** — Ambiguous: tone, formality, call-to-action
6. **"Create a project proposal"** — Ambiguous: scope, format, detail level
7. **"Explain how machine learning works"** — Ambiguous: audience expertise, depth, examples
8. **"Write a social media post about our product"** — Ambiguous: platform, tone, length
9. **"Help me prepare for a job interview"** — Ambiguous: role level, company type, question focus
10. **"Review my code and suggest improvements"** — Ambiguous: focus areas, style preferences, priority

## B.3 Post-Task Survey Questions

1. "In your own words, what decisions did the AI make on your behalf?" (open-ended)
2. "Were there any points where the AI could have asked for clarification but didn't?" (Y/N + explanation)
3. "Rate how well the output matched your intent" (1-7 scale)
4. "Rate your sense of control over the interaction" (1-7 scale)

## B.4 Coding Procedure

1. Two coders independently review each transcript
2. For each AI response, identify all implicit assumptions
3. Classify each assumption by IPP type (Scope, Temporal, Entity, Authority, Form, Abstraction)
4. Compare user-reported assumptions to coder-identified assumptions
5. Calculate inter-rater reliability (Cohen's κ)
6. Resolve disagreements through discussion; document resolution rationale

# Appendix C: Construct Measurement Instruments

## C.1 IPP Annotation Codebook

**Definition:**
An Intent Pivot Point is any moment in an interaction where the AI could reasonably interpret the user's intent in two or more materially different ways.

**Decision Rules:**
1. Would a different interpretation change the output meaningfully? If no, not an IPP.
2. Is the correct interpretation explicitly stated by the user? If yes, not an IPP.
3. Could a reasonable person interpret this differently? If yes, potential IPP.

**Classification:**
- **SCOPE:** Boundaries of the request are ambiguous
- **TEMPORAL:** Time reference is ambiguous
- **ENTITY:** Which entity is referenced is ambiguous
- **AUTHORITY:** Whose perspective to adopt is ambiguous
- **FORM:** Output format or structure is ambiguous
- **ABSTRACTION:** Level of detail is ambiguous

## C.2 Intent Fidelity Index (IFI) Instrument

**Semantic Alignment (Expert Rating):**
Rate the correspondence between the user's stated intent and the AI output:
1 = No correspondence; 4 = Moderate correspondence; 7 = Perfect correspondence

**Constraint Preservation:**
List all constraints stated by user. For each, mark: Satisfied / Violated / Not Applicable
Score = (Satisfied) / (Satisfied + Violated) × 100

**Subjective Satisfaction:**
"The AI output matches what I actually wanted" — 1 (Strongly Disagree) to 7 (Strongly Agree)

## C.3 ICD Perception Scale

Post-task items (7-point Likert):
1. "I felt in control of the AI's interpretation of my request"
2. "The AI asked for clarification when needed"
3. "I could have directed the AI differently if I wanted to"
4. "The AI made decisions that should have been mine to make" (reverse-scored)
