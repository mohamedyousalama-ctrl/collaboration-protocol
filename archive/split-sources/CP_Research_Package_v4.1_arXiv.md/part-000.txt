# 1. Abstract

Human-AI collaboration failures persist even when AI systems are technically capable. We argue these failures often stem from **Silent Intent Inference**—a structural flaw where AI resolves semantic ambiguity without explicit user authorization. We frame this not as interaction friction but as an **AI safety risk**: it creates accountability gaps, untraceable decisions, and systematic erosion of user agency.

We introduce the **Collaboration Protocol (CP)**, a theoretical framework preserving human semantic authority through three operationalized constructs:

- **Intent Control Degree (ICD):** A user-adjustable parameter (0.0–1.0) governing how ambiguity is resolved
- **Intent Pivot Points (IPP):** Semantic locations where meaning may branch, with a six-type taxonomy
- **Intent Fidelity Index (IFI):** A composite metric measuring intent preservation across interactions

Each construct includes specific measurement procedures enabling empirical validation. We propose a phased research agenda: (1) quantifying silent inference prevalence in current LLM systems, (2) validating ICD effects on collaboration quality, and (3) establishing IPP annotation reliability.

**This paper establishes a falsifiable theoretical foundation.** It does not report empirical findings but provides the conceptual infrastructure—operationalized definitions, measurement instruments, and ready-to-run study protocols—for the research community to validate or refute its claims. If the proposed studies fail, the theory is wrong; if they succeed, the path to intent-governed human-AI collaboration becomes clearer.

# 2. Introduction

## 2.1 Motivation

Modern AI systems routinely produce outputs that are:
- Technically correct
- Linguistically coherent
- Aligned with training objectives

Yet still **wrong for the user**.

This gap cannot be explained solely by model alignment, prompt quality, or user error. Instead, it reflects a deeper protocol-level failure: AI systems silently resolve ambiguity without verifying human intent. We term this phenomenon **Silent Intent Inference**.

## 2.2 Illustrative Failure Case

Consider the following observed interaction:

> **User:** What is the capital of Egypt?  
> **AI:** Cairo  
> **User:** How many population?  
> **AI:** 112 million

Post-hoc analysis reveals that the AI inferred the second question referred to **Egypt** (population ~112 million) rather than **Cairo** (population ~22 million), based on conversational proximity rather than explicit user intent. While this inference was reasonable, it was **unauthorized**.
