# 11. Conclusion

Silent Intent Inference is a structural flaw in modern human-AI interaction—and a safety risk that creates accountability gaps, untraceable decisions, and systematic user disempowerment. Current systems resolve ambiguity invisibly, displacing meaning ownership from humans to machines. This paper introduces the Collaboration Protocol—a principled framework that preserves human semantic authority through explicit construct governance.

We have defined four operationalized constructs (ICD, IPP, IFI, and Intent Decay), specified seven protocol laws, and designed four empirical studies to validate core claims. The 30-day pilot protocol enables immediate research action.

The contribution is not a complete solution but a **falsifiable framework**: if the studies fail, the theory is wrong; if they succeed, the path to better human-AI collaboration is clearer.

The Collaboration Protocol does not limit AI capability—it channels that capability through human intent. In an era of increasingly powerful AI systems, this channeling may prove essential to ensuring that AI serves human purposes rather than substituting for them.

Beyond academic validation, the framework opens pathways to practical application: regulatory auditing of intent compliance (Appendix E) and organizational intent signatures (Appendix F) demonstrate how CP principles can scale to institutional adoption. **Meaning ownership**—the right of humans to define acceptable interpretations—may emerge as a foundational principle for AI governance, aligning with existing consent principles, explainability requirements, and accountability law trajectories.
