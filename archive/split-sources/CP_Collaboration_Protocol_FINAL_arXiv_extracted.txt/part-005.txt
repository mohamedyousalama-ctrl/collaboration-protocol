6 Discussion
6.1 Theoretical Implications
The Collaboration Protocol contributes to human-AI interaction theory by identifying a previously
unnamed failure modeSilent Intent Inferenceand positioning it as a protocol-level rather than
capability-level problem. This reframing has signicant implications.
First, it suggests that improvements in model capability alone will not resolve collaboration
failures if the underlying interaction protocol permits unauthorized inference. A more capable
model making silent inferences may produce more sophisticated errors that are harder to detect.
Second, the framework introduces semantic authority as a rst-class concern in human-AI interaction, complementing existing work on task allocation, explanation, and trust. This lls a gap
in the theoretical landscape.
Third, the operationalization of constructs (ICD, IPP, IFI) provides concrete mechanisms for
translating abstract principles into measurable system behaviors, enabling empirical research on
intent preservation.
6.2 Practical Implications
For system designers, the framework oers concrete guidance: implement adjustable ICD controls,
surface detected IPPs before generation, maintain audit trails of intent transformations, and preserve
user override capability throughout interaction.
For organizations deploying AI systems in high-stakes contexts, CP compliance criteria provide
an auditable standard for intent governance. The framework supports accountability requirements
by making inference behavior traceable.
For users, the Intent Control Degree concept provides a mental model for understanding and
controlling AI behaviormoving beyond the binary of trust the AI or avoid the AI toward
nuanced calibration.

6.3 IPP Logging for Enterprise Accountability
A signicant practical contribution of the Collaboration Protocol is the IPP Loga structured audit trail capturing every intent-critical decision made during human-AI interaction. For enterprises
deploying AI in regulated or high-stakes contexts (legal, medical, nancial), this log addresses a fundamental accountability gap: the inability to reconstruct why an AI system produced a particular
output.
Log Structure. Each IPP Log entry captures:
 Timestamp and session ID: When and where the decision occurred
 Original query: The user's input at the decision point
 Detected IPP: The ambiguous element and its type (Scope, Temporal, etc.)
 Materiality score: The computed M(a) value from Equation 2
 Context Root: The source of semantic anchoring (intra-session, cross-session, environmental,
or null), including source location, anchor text, and condence level
 Context Factor: The CF value indicating whether prior context resolved (< 1.0) or amplied
(> 1.0) the ambiguity
 Interpretations considered: The semantic alternatives the system identied
 Resolution method: Whether surfaced to user (and their choice) or resolved silently (with ICD
justication)
 Final interpretation: The meaning adopted for generation
Accountability Applications. The IPP Log enables several enterprise-critical functions:
1. Audit trails: Compliance teams can review exactly which semantic decisions were made and
whether users authorized themessential for regulated industries.
2. Dispute resolution: When AI outputs cause harm, the log provides evidence of whether the
system followed protocol (surfaced material IPPs) or failed (silent inference on critical ambiguity).
3. Continuous improvement: Aggregated logs reveal patternswhich query types generate the
most IPPs, which resolutions users most frequently overrideinforming system renement.
4. Legal defensibility: Organizations can demonstrate due diligence by showing their AI systems
implement intent governance with documented decision trails.
Implementation. IPP Logs can be stored as structured JSON, integrated with existing enterprise logging infrastructure, and retained according to organizational data policies. The logging
overhead is minimal (metadata only, not full conversation storage) and can be implemented independently of the IPP detection algorithm itself.
This accountability infrastructure transforms the Collaboration Protocol from a theoretical
framework into a practical compliance tooladdressing the growing regulatory focus on AI transparency and explainability.

6.4 Limitations
Several limitations bound the current contribution:
No empirical validation yet. The framework's claims are hypotheses requiring the proposed
studies. Until validated, they remain theoretical assertions.
Ecological validity concerns. Lab tasks may not capture real-world complexity. Field studies
with professional users are needed.
IPP boundary cases. Despite the taxonomy, edge cases may resist clean classication. Reliability testing will reveal construct boundaries.
