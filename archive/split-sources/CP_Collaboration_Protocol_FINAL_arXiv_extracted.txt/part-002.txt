4 The Collaboration Protocol Framework
4.1 Core Constructs
The Collaboration Protocol denes four primary constructs with operational denitions enabling
empirical measurement.
4.1.1 Intent
Intent is the human-authored constraint determining acceptable outcomes. Intent species not
what the AI should produce but what boundaries production must respect: meaning boundaries
(acceptable interpretations), constraint sets (what must/must not be true), and contextual assumptions (what the user takes for granted).
For measurement, intent is captured through pre-task articulation (user states outcome and
constraints), post-task validation (user conrms match), and counterfactual probing (user identies
acceptable alternatives).
Boundary Conditions. Intent is distinct from: (a) preferenceintent denes acceptable
boundaries, not optimal choices within those boundaries; (b) instructionintent constrains meaning, while instructions specify actions; (c) goalintent may include unstated assumptions and
contextual knowledge beyond explicit objectives. A user may have clear intent without being able
to fully articulate it; the framework aims to surface this gap, not eliminate it.
4.1.2 Intent Pivot Points (IPP)
An Intent Pivot Point exists when: (1) two or more interpretations are plausible; (2) resolution
materially changes the output; and (3) the user has not specied which interpretation to use.
Boundary Conditions. Not every ambiguity constitutes an IPP. An ambiguity qualies as
an IPP only if resolution materially aects the outputminor variations in phrasing or formatting
that preserve meaning are not IPPs. Similarly, ambiguities where all plausible resolutions lead to
equivalent outcomes are not IPPs. The materiality threshold is context-dependent: in legal or
medical domains, smaller variations may be material; in casual conversation, larger variations may
be immaterial.
