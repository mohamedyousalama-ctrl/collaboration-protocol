Clarication Modalities. Pivot surfacing need not mean disruptive prompts. Alternatives
include: (1) highlighting ambiguous phrases in user input before generation begins, inviting preemptive clarication; (2) inline annotation during output, marking assumptions for post-hoc review; (3)

User Authority with System Guidance. ICD remains under user control at all times.
However, CP-compliant systems may suggest ICD levels based on detected task characteristics
(document type, domain indicators, ambiguity density). Users may accept, modify, or ignore such
suggestions. This preserves human sovereignty while enabling helpful defaults.
Implementation Feasibility. ICD could integrate with existing UI patterns: a slider in
ChatGPT's custom instructions, a preset selector in Claude's system prompt conguration, or an
inline toggle within conversation interfaces. Latency considerations suggest IPP detection could
operate in two modes: (1) pre-generation scan ( 100-500ms overhead) that identies ambiguous
elements before output begins, and (2) progressive disclosure where potential IPPs are highlighted
in real-time as the user types. Modern inference infrastructure can accommodate such overhead for
high-ICD tasks where users have explicitly requested thorough ambiguity handling.

Intent Decay: Partial Solution, Open Problem. A critical phenomenon requiring further
research is intent decaythe gradual loss, distortion, or drift of original user intent over extended
interactions. We identify six contributing factors and assess how the current framework addresses
each:
1. Accumulated inference errors: Small silent assumptions compound across turns. CP addresses this through IPP surfacing, which prevents silent accumulation by making inferences
explicit at decision points.

addresses this through IPP surfacing for major semantic branches, though subtle drift may pass
undetected.
6. Feedback loop distortion: AI outputs may shape subsequent user inputs, creating circular
inuence. CP does not address thismodeling how AI outputs inuence user intent formation
remains beyond current scope.

7 Conclusion
Silent Intent Inference is a structural pattern in modern human-AI interactionan accountability challenge creating oversight gaps, dicult-to-trace decisions, and potential reduction in user
agency. Current systems often resolve ambiguity invisibly, shifting semantic authority from humans
to machines.
This paper introduces the Collaboration Protocol, a framework preserving human semantic authority through explicit governance mechanisms. We dene four operationalized constructs (Intent,
ICD, IPP, IFI), specify seven protocol laws, and design four validation studies.
The contribution is a falsiable framework: if the proposed studies fail to conrm hypotheses,
the theory requires revision; if they succeed, the path to intent-governed human-AI collaboration
becomes clearer.
