4.1.7 Intent Fidelity Index (IFI)
Intent Fidelity Index measures how well output preserves original intent:
IFI = 0.4(SA) + 0.3(CP) + 0.3(SS) (3)
Where SA = expert-rated semantic alignment (17, normalized), CP = proportion of stated
constraints satised (01), and SS = user satisfaction rating (17, normalized).
Weight Justication. The proposed weights reect a theoretical prioritization grounded in
the framework's core premise: intent delity is primarily about meaning preservation. Semantic
Accuracy (SA) receives the highest weight (0.4) because it captures whether the output means
what the user intendedthe fundamental measure of intent delity. Constraint Preservation (CP)
and Subjective Satisfaction (SS) receive equal weights (0.3 each) as complementary validity checks:
CP veries that explicit requirements were honored, while SS captures holistic user judgment that
may detect intent violations missed by formal measures.
This weighting scheme treats SA as necessary but not sucient: an output could be semantically
aligned yet violate explicit constraints (low CP) or feel o to users in ways experts miss (low SS).
The equal weighting of CP and SS reects uncertainty about their relative importance, which varies
by domaintechnical tasks may weight CP higher, while creative tasks may weight SS higher.
We emphasize that these weights are provisional. Study 4 in our validation agenda includes
weight sensitivity analysis, and we anticipate domain-specic calibration. The framework's contribution is the structure of IFI (three complementary components), not the specic numerical weights,
which remain empirically renable.
4.2 Protocol Laws
The Collaboration Protocol establishes seven governing principles:

Law 1: Maximum Capacity. AI systems operate at full capacity unless explicitly constrained
by human intent.
Law 2: Intent Fidelity. No output is valid unless it preserves highest achievable delity to human
intent.
Law 3: Articulation Supremacy. Intent articulation is human-owned. AI may assist but must
not decide intent.
Law 4: Human Ownership of Ambiguity. Ambiguity belongs to the human by default.
Law 5: AI Non-Assumption. AI must not silently resolve intent-critical ambiguity.
Law 6: Human Override. Humans may intervene at any Intent Pivot Point.
Law 7: Reversibility. All intent transformations must be reversible by the human.

4.4 Compliance Criteria
A system is CP-compliant if it: exposes IPPs according to user's ICD setting; respects user choices
at all surfaced pivots; allows human override at any stage; prevents silent resolution of intent-critical
ambiguity; and maintains traceable logs of intent transformations.
5 Proposed Validation Studies
We propose four studies to validate the framework's core claims.
5.1 Study 1: Silent Inference Prevalence
Research Question: How frequently do current AI systems engage in Silent Intent Inference?
Hypotheses: H1a: Silent inferences occur in >60% of complex tasks. H1b: Users are unaware
in >70% of cases.
Method: 200 knowledge workers complete 5 complex tasks (recommendation letter, project proposal, data analysis, creative brief, technical explanation) using ChatGPT or Claude. Four trained
annotators identify assumptions using IPP codebook. Post-task survey captures user-reported assumptions.
Analysis: Silent Inference Rate = coded assumptions / total interactions. Awareness Rate =
user-identied / coder-identied assumptions. Inter-rater reliability via Cohen's κ.
