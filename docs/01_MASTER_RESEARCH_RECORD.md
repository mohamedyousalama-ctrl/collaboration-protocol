**Scope:** Theory, terminology, frozen protocol, research agenda, implementation work, empirical materials, applied extensions, provenance, and current research frontier.

## 1. Research program in one paragraph

The Collaboration Protocol (CP) research program investigates how human authority over meaning and action can remain explicit and reconstructible when people work with AI systems. The earliest recovered research lineage names **Silent Intent Inference (SII)**: a machine-generated assumption about what a user means, made without explicit human authorization, that materially affects the output. That lineage develops a vocabulary for locating, controlling, measuring, and logging semantic branching: **Intent Pivot Points (IPP), Intent Control Degree (ICD), Intent Fidelity Index (IFI), Context Roots (CR), Context Factor (CF), Semantic Materiality Proxy (SMP), and IPP logging**. A later frozen system-model lineage recasts CP as an execution-time accountability protocol with first-class **Context, Intent, Agent, Node, and Pivot** objects, a five-link **Responsibility Chain**, a read-only **Guardian**, explicit **Friction**, six state stores, and append-only logging. Subsequent work tests and extends these ideas in financial and health AI, Klear, naturalistic incident extraction, personal continuity/Ghost, and current research on continuous authorization across state changes and multi-agent execution.

This repository preserves all of those lineages while refusing to pretend they are one perfectly consistent version.

## 2. Central intellectual problem: semantic authority

### 2.1 Silent Intent Inference

The January 2026 research package defines **Silent Intent Inference** as:

> Any machine-generated assumption about user intent made without explicit human authorization, where the assumption materially affects the output.

The four elements in the recovered research package are:

1. the assumption is generated by the machine;
2. it concerns the user's intent rather than an ordinary factual proposition;
3. the user did not explicitly authorize the interpretive choice; and
4. the choice materially changes the output.

The key theoretical move is to treat misinterpretation not only as a model-quality problem but as an **authority and accountability problem**. The research question becomes not merely “did the model guess correctly?” but “who had authority to resolve the ambiguity, was that authority explicit, and can the decision be reconstructed?”

### 2.2 The canonical early illustrative case

The early paper lineage repeatedly uses a short example:

```text
User: What is the capital of Egypt?
AI: Cairo
User: How many population?
AI: 112 million
```

The model has implicitly selected Egypt rather than Cairo as the referent of the second question. The answer may be factually defensible for one interpretation, yet the interpretive branch itself was not surfaced. The early CP theory uses this to distinguish **semantic correctness under one reading** from **authorized meaning selection**.

### 2.3 Semantic authority

Across the research lineage, “semantic authority” means the authority to determine what a materially underspecified human instruction is taken to mean. Earlier CP work focuses on authority over interpretation; frozen CP v1.0.1 expands the accountability problem from interpretation to **verified intent, valid context, scoped execution, and reconstructible action**.

## 3. Early CP theory lineage: SII / IPP / ICD / IFI / CR

The January 2026 research-package lineage should be read as a theoretical and empirical-research program distinct from the later frozen v1.0.1 object model.

### 3.1 Intent Pivot Point (IPP)

An **Intent Pivot Point** is a semantic point at which:

- at least two interpretations are plausible;
- choosing among them materially changes the output; and
- the user has not specified which interpretation should govern.

The early six-type taxonomy is:

| Type | Research meaning |
|---|---|
| Scope | Boundaries of the requested work are underspecified. |
| Temporal | Time reference is underspecified. |
| Entity Reference | The referenced person/object/entity is uncertain. |
| Authority | Whose perspective, mandate, or basis should govern is uncertain. |
| Output Form | The requested structure or form is underspecified. |
| Abstraction Level | Desired depth/granularity is underspecified. |

July 2026 corpus work later proposed additional phenomena and candidate taxonomy extensions; these are documented separately and are not retroactively inserted into the original six-type taxonomy.

### 3.2 Intent Control Degree (ICD)

The early framework proposes **Intent Control Degree**, a user-selected 0.0–1.0 control over how aggressively the system may resolve ambiguity without interruption. Historical documents describe low ICD as greater model discretion and ICD 1.0 as surfacing all material ambiguity for explicit confirmation.

This construct is a theory/prototype mechanism, not part of the frozen CP v1.0.1 object model.

### 3.3 Intent Fidelity Index (IFI)

The early research package proposes a composite **Intent Fidelity Index**:

```text
IFI = 0.4(SA) + 0.3(CP) + 0.3(SS)
```

where the historical documents define:

- **SA — Semantic Alignment**: expert-rated correspondence between stated intent and output meaning;
- **CP — Constraint Preservation**: proportion of user-specified constraints satisfied; and
- **SS — Subjective Satisfaction**: user-reported match to intent.

The formula is a proposed measurement construct. It is not a frozen v1.0.1 accountability metric, and the frozen v1.0 evaluation framework explicitly excludes user satisfaction and optimization claims.

### 3.4 Context Roots (CR)

Later early-lineage work introduces **Context Roots** to anchor ambiguity in prior interaction history. Recovered material describes four root classes:

- intra-session;
- cross-session;
- environmental; and
- null/no prior context.

Each root may carry source location, anchor text, confidence, and staleness. Context Roots are not the same thing as the frozen v1.0.1 **Context** object. The terminology collision is material and is preserved in the terminology index.

### 3.5 Context Factor (CF)

The **Context Factor** adjusts ambiguity materiality based on the quality of prior context. Historical values are:

| CF | Historical interpretation |
|---:|---|
| 0.50 | strong prior context resolves ambiguity |
| 0.75 | partial context provides useful hints |
| 1.00 | no relevant prior context |
| 1.25 | weak or stale prior context increases uncertainty |
| 1.50 | conflicting prior contexts amplify ambiguity |

These values are proposal-level parameters, not empirically calibrated constants.

### 3.6 Semantic Materiality Proxy (SMP)

The early paper proposes the following materiality score:

```text
M(a) = [alpha * D_sem + beta * C_mag + gamma * (1 - R)] * CF(a)
```

with historical default weights `alpha=0.4`, `beta=0.4`, `gamma=0.2`, where:

- `D_sem` = semantic distance among plausible interpretations;
- `C_mag` = consequence magnitude;
- `R` = reversibility; and
- `CF(a)` = Context Factor.

Historical thresholds classify ambiguities as Critical, Significant, or Minor. The recovered paper explicitly describes the weights and thresholds as estimates requiring empirical calibration.

### 3.7 IPP logging

A major applied idea in the early lineage is the **IPP Log**: a structured record of material interpretive decisions. Historical descriptions include the original query, detected IPP, candidate interpretations, materiality, context root, resolution method, adopted interpretation, and timestamps. The underlying contribution is to make interpretation itself an auditable event rather than only logging the final output.

### 3.8 Intent Decay

**Intent Decay** appears as a theoretical concept for cumulative divergence caused by compounded assumptions. The research package explicitly defers a formal mathematical model until empirical data exists.

### 3.9 Intent Signatures and constitutional onboarding

The historical future-work program proposes:

- **Intent Signatures** — recurring intent patterns intended to reduce repeated setup;
- a persistent **constitutional onboarding** model — durable user-configured defaults such as risk posture and surfacing preferences.

These ideas later influence Project Continuity/Ghost work. They are not frozen v1.0.1 semantics, which explicitly exclude learning preferences across sessions and latent-goal inference.

## 4. Frozen CP v1.0.1: interaction-level accountability protocol

The frozen lineage defines CP as an execution-time mediation layer between user signals and system actions. Its core is not a model personality or prompt. It is a state-and-gate architecture intended to make consequential execution dependent on explicit, inspectable authority.

### 4.1 First-class objects

**Context** — bounded, user-declared semantic and operational scope. Contexts may be hierarchical, inspectable, and constrained. Implicit context merging is forbidden.

**Intent** — explicit, verified declaration of what the user wants the system to do under defined constraints. The System Model states that intent is not inferred from text, behavior, or pattern recognition; it binds to exactly one context and is revocable before execution.

**Agent** — scoped executor or advisor with explicit permissions and prohibitions. An agent cannot self-authorize, escape context, or modify its own permissions.

**Node** — user-committed structural constraint governing future reasoning. It is binding and reversible only through explicit revision.

**Pivot** — user-declared epistemic marker of perceived significance. It is annotative rather than binding and never independently triggers action.

### 4.2 Responsibility Chain

The frozen chain is:

```text
User Signal -> CP Verification -> AI Suggestion -> User Commitment -> Action
```

Every link must be logged; no link may be skipped or compressed; the chain must be reconstructible from logs; and user commitment is the only mechanism that advances a signal to action.

### 4.3 Guardian

The frozen **Guardian** is read-only and issues one of three gate decisions: `Allow`, `Clarify`, or `Refuse`.

Post-classification rules:

- PC-1 ambiguous signal -> Clarify
- PC-2 out of scope -> Clarify
- PC-3 action without context -> Clarify
- PC-4 clear/no action -> Allow

Pre-execution rules:

- PE-1 no Intent -> Refuse
- PE-2 Intent not verified/active -> Refuse
- PE-3 Intent/action mismatch -> Refuse
- PE-4 Context not open -> Refuse
- PE-5 Agent not allowed in Context -> Refuse
- PE-6 Agent not active -> Refuse
- PE-7 Agent type prohibits action -> Refuse
- PE-8 action exceeds Agent permissions -> Refuse
- PE-9 action violates Context constraints -> Refuse
- PE-10 unresolved Friction -> Refuse
- PE-11 all checks pass -> Allow

### 4.4 Friction

Frozen triggers:

`ambiguous_signal`, `unverified_intent`, `scope_boundary`, `permission_violation`, `high_risk_action`, `missing_context`, `conflict_detected`.

Frozen resolutions:

`user_clarified`, `user_confirmed`, `user_revised`, `user_abandoned`, `system_blocked`.

Friction cannot be silently auto-resolved by the system.

### 4.5 Stores

The frozen model defines six stores: Context, Intent, Node (including Pivot), Action, Friction, and append-only Log.

### 4.6 Frozen non-goals

CP v1.0.1 explicitly does **not**:

- model long-term cognition/belief evolution;
- infer hidden/latent/subconscious goals;
- optimize outcomes, performance, or correctness;
- learn preferences across sessions;
- predict future intent;
- replace institutional/legal/organizational governance; or
- claim alignment/safety/ethics guarantees beyond interaction accountability.

That scope boundary is essential when later Continuity/Ghost work is discussed.

## 5. Frozen evaluation program

The recovered evaluation framework limits itself to interaction-level accountability and states that it does not measure optimization, efficiency, or user satisfaction.

Core evaluation claims are:

- no action without verified Intent;
- Context boundaries enforced;
- actions traceable to human decisions;
- ambiguity becomes Friction rather than silent progression;
- accountability reconstructible; and
- Agents cannot self-authorize or escape scope.

The program proposes:

- instrumented log metrics;
- CP-off vs CP-on comparison;
- trace reconstruction; and
- injected failures for ambiguity, scope, unverified intent, and agent permission violations.

The recovered `v1_0_1_validation_tests.md` remains an **unexecuted validation template** in the available archive.

## 7. Klear: applied implementation and financial comparative study

Klear is an important applied CP project, but the source audit shows it is not a clean implementation of the frozen v1.0.1 reference semantics.

Recovered Klear code combines:

- early ICD/IPP/IFI/Context Roots language;
- Safety Floor logic;
- Guardian-like model calls;
- streaming monitoring / mid-generation concepts;
- friction and audit/report objects; and
- finance-domain restrictions.

Important implementation findings preserved by this archive include fail-open paths on Guardian/monitor errors, retry paths that skip parts of the normal CP1 pipeline, and a hard-coded `responsibility_chain_intact: true` reporting value. The implementation therefore constitutes historical/applied evidence, not a conformance oracle.

### Financial evaluation

The preserved Klear workbooks document financial-domain comparisons involving Klear, GPT-4o, and Gemini 1.5 Pro, with categories for research, boundary pressure, credentials, drift, stress, and audit traceability.

The workbook generations contain **different denominators and aggregate summaries**. For example, one later workbook reports an overall Klear pass rate of 96% using a 26/27 framing while another generation reports 94% and another reports 22/23 or related completion counts. The archive therefore preserves the workbooks rather than selecting a preferred number. No consolidated publication result should be stated until the row-level dataset and denominators are reconciled.

The workbooks nevertheless provide important implementation evidence: Klear was intentionally tested for boundary enforcement, credential challenges, scope holds, Safety Floor activation, and audit-chain visibility. They also record known Klear defects such as false-positive scope warnings and borderline output after scope expansion.

## 8. Health-domain extension and Safety Floor

The health extension arose from a reported IntentHealth pilot anomaly: intent-fidelity scoring and clinical-safety judgment could diverge. The response of the research program was to define a **Safety Floor (SF)** outside ordinary user-controlled ambiguity resolution.

Historical extension constructs include:

- **Safety Floor (SF)** — domain-validated minimum assertions intended to be non-suppressible;
- **Safety Compliance Score (SCS)**;
- **IFI-Health** — a health-specific composite adding safety compliance;
- **Law 8 — Domain Safety Non-Negotiability**; and
- audit events such as `SF_ACTIVATED`, `SF_INJECTED`, and `SF_VIOLATION`.

This work is a **domain extension**, not part of the frozen v1.0.1 core. Its clinical claims require qualified human validation; the recovered specification itself says Safety Floor content must be clinically reviewed and that domain-classification accuracy remains a limitation.

## 9. Later CP / product applications: Ghost and Project Continuity

August 2026 product/research work applies CP ideas to a broader continuity system. The key constitutional separation is:

> Memory is not authority.

The Continuity/Ghost program explores persistent context, a personal Presence, cross-tool continuity, and governed action. It explicitly treats frozen CP as the **authority plane**, while memory, inferred context, and continuity remain outside CP until confirmed into valid operational state.

Applied concepts preserved in the source archive include:

- Personal Constitution;
- confirmed vs inferred Context;
- Context Capsule / continuity state;
- “Come” invocation / Presence;
- Meaning Ledger;
- Contextual Action Plan;
- Lead, Listener, and Scoped Participant roles;
- Responsibility Flow;
- Decision Packets; and
- a principle that one human intention may coordinate many work units while each target retains separate Context, authority, evidence, and recovery.

These concepts are derivative product/research work. They must not be cited as frozen CP v1.0.1 semantics.

## 10. Current research frontier: authorization continuity after approval

By August 2026 the research question had shifted from “does the model clarify ambiguity?” toward a harder agentic-systems question:

> **Does the action eventually executed remain inside the exact authority the human granted, even after time, state change, data change, permission change, context change, or multi-agent handoff?**

This yields a working distinction:

- **Final confirmation** is a snapshot at a particular time.
- **Authorization integrity** is a property that must remain true at execution time.

The current research program proposes deliberately **zero-ambiguity** benchmarks in which the original human instruction and approval are perfectly explicit. Failures are injected only after approval: stale state, payload/version changes, permission revocation, context collision, handoff corruption, unresolved exception, constraint change, or stale approval reuse.

Candidate research terms and metrics include:

- **Execution-State Authorization Integrity (ESAI)**;
- **End-to-End Authorization Fidelity (EAF)**;
- **Semantic Authority Continuity (SAC)**;
- **Unauthorized Financial State Mutation Rate (UFSMR)**;
- **Stale Approval Reuse Rate**;
- **Cross-Context Contamination Rate**; and
- **Responsibility Reconstruction Rate**.

These are **working hypotheses and proposed measurements**, not frozen terminology and not established results.

The strongest causal hypothesis currently worth testing is:

> When a human-authorized operation passes through changing state, multiple AI agents, and external systems, a CP-governed execution layer can preserve the exact boundary of human authority through execution more reliably than architectures that rely only on a prior confirmation, prompt-level instruction, or static approval.

This hypothesis must be tested against strong baselines such as RBAC/policy/approval/audit architectures, not merely raw chat models.

## 13. Research integrity rules established by the program

Across the source documents, several working principles recur and are retained as methodological commitments:

1. Do not fabricate statistics or experiments.
2. Distinguish proposed studies from completed studies.
3. Preserve denominator and source uncertainty rather than choosing a convenient result.
4. Treat source artifacts and raw data as higher authority than narrative summaries.
5. Do not let later papers silently redefine a frozen protocol.
6. Use explicit limitations at the time a claim is created.
7. Separate model assistance in extraction from researcher-only ground-truth classification.
8. Keep domain safety requirements conceptually distinct from intent fidelity.
9. Test CP against strong conventional governance baselines, not deliberately weak competitors.
10. Preserve negative findings and implementation defects because they define the true boundary of the theory.
